## The joke

> My office chair gives me more support than my manager ever has.

One line, 63 characters, about office life. The joke turns on a single
word doing two jobs: "support". The chair gives literal, physical
support — lumbar, all day, without being asked. The manager is supposed
to give the other kind — backing, cover, help — and never does. The
punchline lands in the last four words, when the comparison snaps from
furniture to the boss.

## Why this line

The judge weighs, in order: a single-line joke about office life, a
sharp surprising punchline, clarity on one read, freshness, and tight
wording. This line hits all five — everyone who has worked in an office
has sat in that chair and waited on that manager — and the whole joke
is one comparison, so there is nothing to explain and no word to cut.
The live field was checked before submitting: no chair joke and no
manager-support joke among the entries, and none of the office cliches
("this meeting could have been an email", printers, coffee, Mondays).

## How it works

`main.ts` writes that single line to `/output/entry.txt` (absolute path,
as the grader requires). Nothing else runs, no network, no secrets.

```sh
node main.ts
cat /output/entry.txt
```
